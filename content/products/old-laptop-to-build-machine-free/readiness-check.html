Can your old laptop become a build machine?

A five-minute check before you spend an afternoon on it.

That laptop in the drawer might be one free afternoon away from being a real computer for building things — writing code, making small projects, and putting a website on the internet. Or it might not be worth it. This quick check tells you which, before you commit any time.

The five-minute check

If you can tick all six, your laptop is a good candidate:

  • It powers on and starts up reliably, and holds a charge.
  • The screen, keyboard, and trackpad all work (or you have an external one to plug in).
  • It has at least 2 GB of RAM (4 GB or more is much nicer).
  • It has about 20 GB of free storage.
  • You have a USB flash drive, 8 GB or larger, you don't mind erasing.
  • You have internet and a couple of hours (you can split it up).

Age matters less than most people expect — Linux runs well on machines that current Windows or macOS have left behind.

One important warning

The full process installs a fresh operating system, which erases everything currently on the laptop. Anything you want to keep must be backed up somewhere else first. The full guide walks through exactly how — but know this going in.

Clear stop signs

Skip it (or fix these first) if:

  • The laptop won't power on, won't charge, or the screen stays black — that's a hardware fault, and installing Linux won't fix it.
  • You need what's on it and have nowhere to back it up yet. Sort the backup out first.
  • It's the only computer you rely on daily and you can't risk changing it. Use a spare instead.
  • You want a phone-style, zero-effort setup. This is hands-on and takes a couple of focused hours.

What the full process gets you

Following the complete walkthrough, you finish with:

  • A laptop running Linux, updated and secured.
  • The tools developers use — a code editor, Git, Python, Node.js — installed and working.
  • A small website you built by hand.
  • That website live on the internet, at a real address you can share.
  • A clear idea of what to build next.
The honest version

This won't fix a broken laptop, and it isn't a shortcut to a job or an income. It's a genuine, complete path from "I have an old laptop" to "I have a working computer with a live project on it." That's worth a lot on its own.
